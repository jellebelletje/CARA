## 1. Leadership and sponsorship

*Whether anyone with authority is visibly behind this, and whether managers model the behaviour they are asking for.*

### lead-01 · pilot-critical

**Is there a named executive sponsor with authority over the pilot cohort's priorities and budget?**

Not whether leadership is supportive, but whether you can write a person's name down. Microsoft's planning checklist makes this task one, and an unnamed sponsor is the most common silent failure.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody can name a sponsor, or the person named has no authority over the cohort and has never been asked. |
| Emerging | A sponsor is named but has not been briefed, has said nothing publicly, or cannot actually release the cohort's time. |
| Ready | A named sponsor has agreed the pilot in writing, can release the cohort's time and budget, and has a speaking slot at launch. |
| Scalable | The sponsor is accountable for a stated AI outcome that outlives this pilot, and other leaders defer to them on it. |

- **If Not ready:** Identify a sponsor with real authority over the cohort's time and budget, and secure their agreement in writing before any licence is assigned. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Brief the named sponsor and secure one visible commitment from them, such as opening the launch session. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption planning checklist](https://adoption.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/essential-guide/plan/)

### lead-02

**Can leadership state why Copilot, why now, in terms of a business goal rather than "AI is important"?**

Participants who cannot answer "why are we doing this" default to treating the pilot as an IT experiment they are free to ignore.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The pilot is described in terms of the technology, with no business goal attached to it at all. |
| Emerging | A rationale exists but it is generic AI ambition, not specific to what this organisation is trying to achieve this year. |
| Ready | Leaders can state the business goal the pilot serves and connect it to something the cohort already cares about. |
| Scalable | The rationale is written down, repeated consistently by more than one leader, and the cohort can play it back unprompted. |

- **If Not ready:** Run a one-hour framing session with the sponsor to produce a written why-now statement tied to a current business goal. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Sharpen the rationale into cohort-specific language and have the sponsor deliver it at launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### lead-03 · pilot-critical

**Do the pilot cohort's line managers visibly use Copilot themselves, in front of their teams?**

Work Trend Index 2026 found organisational factors carry roughly twice the AI impact of individual factors, and Frontier Professionals have a manager who openly uses AI 85% of the time against 64% for their peers. This is the strongest lever available before launch.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | No manager in the cohort holds a licence, or managers have delegated Copilot entirely to their teams. |
| Emerging | Some managers are licensed and curious, but nothing is visible to their teams. No demos, no shared prompts, no mention in team meetings. |
| Ready | Named cohort managers have committed to a recurring visible ritual using Copilot, and to sharing what worked. |
| Scalable | Manager modelling is expected practice beyond the cohort, managers set quality standards for AI-assisted output, and peers can name specific examples. |

- **If Not ready:** Licence and brief the cohort's line managers before any end-user launch. A pilot whose managers are absent produces adoption data you cannot trust. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Run a 60-minute manager enablement session and agree one visible weekly ritual per manager. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/05/05/how-frontier-firms-are-rebuilding-the-operating-model-for-the-age-of-ai/)

### lead-04

**Have leaders said out loud what happens to time that Copilot frees up?**

The unspoken headcount question. Left unanswered, people quietly under-report time saved, which corrupts the value measurement you are running the pilot to produce.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Employees have raised the question and leadership has avoided answering it. |
| Emerging | Leaders hold an intention but have not stated it publicly, so the cohort is left to infer the answer. |
| Ready | Leaders have stated publicly what freed time is for, in terms the cohort finds credible. |
| Scalable | The statement is backed by a visible example, such as work leadership has explicitly stopped asking for. |

- **If Not ready:** Agree a leadership position on what freed-up time is for and state it before launch. Silence is read as a headcount answer and suppresses reported time savings. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Have the sponsor state the position publicly at launch rather than leaving it implicit. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### lead-05 · pilot-critical

**Have leaders set an accountability standard for AI-assisted output, covering who owns it and what must be checked before it leaves the building?**

One embarrassing unverified output can kill a pilot politically. Work Trend Index 2026 flags setting quality standards for AI as a Frontier-manager marker, 83% against 57%.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody has said who is answerable for AI-assisted output, and there is no rule about checking it before use. |
| Emerging | There is an informal understanding that people should check their work, but nothing written and nothing specific to AI output. |
| Ready | A written standard states that the human sender owns the output, and names which categories of work require verification before release. |
| Scalable | The standard is embedded in existing quality and review processes rather than living as a separate AI policy, and managers actually apply it. |

- **If Not ready:** Publish a one-page accountability standard covering who owns AI-assisted output and which work requires verification before it leaves the organisation. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Convert the informal understanding into a written standard and have managers reference it at launch. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/05/05/how-frontier-firms-are-rebuilding-the-operating-model-for-the-age-of-ai/)

### lead-06

**Is there budget for enablement, meaning training, champion time and comms, separate from the licence spend?**

Licences get funded and the change work often does not, after which champions are asked to do it in their spare time.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Only licences are funded. Training, champion time and comms have no budget and no allocated hours. |
| Emerging | Some enablement is funded informally, typically by asking people to absorb it inside existing roles. |
| Ready | Enablement has its own budget line or explicitly allocated hours, separate from licence spend. |
| Scalable | Enablement funding is planned across the rollout horizon, not only the pilot window. |

- **If Not ready:** Secure a distinct enablement budget or allocated hours before launch. Champion effort funded from goodwill stops the moment delivery pressure arrives. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Formalise the informal enablement effort into named hours per person for the pilot period. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

### lead-07

**Are IT, security, HR/People and the business function aligned behind this, or is it an IT-initiated project?**

An IT-only pilot can deploy but cannot change behaviour. The absence of HR/People in particular shows up later as no skilling and no manager engagement.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The pilot is IT-initiated, and neither the business function nor HR/People has been engaged. |
| Emerging | Other functions have been informed but hold no stake, no tasks and no representation in the pilot team. |
| Ready | IT, security, HR/People and the business function each have a named representative with tasks in the pilot plan. |
| Scalable | Those functions share a standing forum for AI adoption that outlives this pilot. |

- **If Not ready:** Bring the business function and HR/People into the pilot team with named representatives, or accept that the pilot tests deployment rather than adoption. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Give each function a specific task in the pilot plan so involvement is real rather than nominal. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption planning checklist](https://adoption.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/essential-guide/plan/)

## 2. Use cases and value

*Whether you know what the cohort will actually do with Copilot, and how you will tell afterwards whether it helped.*

### case-01 · pilot-critical

**Have you identified specific role-based scenarios for the pilot cohort, or is the plan "give them licences and see what happens"?**

The single strongest predictor in Microsoft's own enablement guidance. See-what-happens pilots produce anecdotes rather than decisions.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The plan is to assign licences and observe. No scenarios are written down. |
| Emerging | Scenarios exist but describe generic Copilot capabilities such as summarise or draft, rather than named pieces of this cohort's work. |
| Ready | Each role in the cohort has at least one written scenario describing a specific recurring task, in the cohort's own language. |
| Scalable | Scenarios are maintained as a living set, with new ones added from what the cohort discovers in use. |

- **If Not ready:** Run a scenario-mapping workshop with the cohort and produce at least one written scenario per role before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Rewrite the capability list as named tasks from the cohort's actual work, using the Microsoft Scenario Library as a starting point. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft Copilot Scenario Library](https://adoption.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/success-kit/)

### case-02 · pilot-critical

**Is there a baseline for the work those scenarios target, meaning how long it takes, how often it happens, and what it costs today?**

Without a before there is no after, and the baseline must be captured before licences land or it is gone permanently.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | No baseline exists and none is planned. How long the target work takes today is simply unknown. |
| Emerging | There is an estimate from memory or one person's opinion, rather than a measurement. |
| Ready | The target tasks have a recorded baseline for time, frequency or volume, captured before licences land. |
| Scalable | The baseline comes from a source that will still exist after the pilot, so the comparison can be repeated at any point. |

- **If Not ready:** Capture the baseline before any licence is assigned. Once the cohort starts using Copilot the before state is unrecoverable. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Upgrade the estimate to a recorded measurement for at least the top two scenarios. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### case-03

**Have the scenarios been validated with the people who would actually do them, rather than designed by the project team on their behalf?**

Project-team-invented scenarios routinely describe work that does not exist in that shape, and the cohort quietly ignores them.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Scenarios were written by the project team and have never been shown to anyone who does the work. |
| Emerging | Scenarios were shown to the cohort for comment, but nothing changed as a result. |
| Ready | Scenarios were built with people who do the work, and at least one was rejected or rewritten by them. |
| Scalable | The cohort proposes and refines scenarios themselves as a matter of course. |

- **If Not ready:** Test each scenario with someone who actually does that work before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Run a working session where the cohort is empowered to reject scenarios, not only to comment on them. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### case-04 · pilot-critical

**Do the chosen scenarios rely on data Copilot can actually reach, in M365 rather than a legacy system, a shared drive or a PDF archive?**

The most common cause of a disappointing pilot. The scenario is sound but the content is not where Copilot can see it. Cheap to check now, brutal to discover in week three.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The scenarios depend on content Copilot cannot reach, such as a legacy system, a local drive or scanned PDFs. |
| Emerging | Reachability has not been checked, so whether the content is indexed and correctly permissioned is unknown. |
| Ready | Content for each scenario has been confirmed present in M365 and reachable under the cohort's own permissions. |
| Scalable | Content reachability is maintained deliberately, and every new scenario is checked for it before being adopted. |

- **If Not ready:** Reselect or rescope the scenarios onto content Copilot can reach, or move the content into M365 first. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Test each scenario against real content using a real cohort account before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### case-05

**Is there a stated value hypothesis per scenario, naming what improves, for whom, and roughly by how much?**

A hypothesis you can be wrong about. This is the pilot's actual question, written down in advance so it cannot be retrofitted to whatever the data shows.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | No expectation has been stated, so any result can be declared a success afterwards. |
| Emerging | There is a general expectation of time saving, with no figure and no named beneficiary. |
| Ready | Each priority scenario has a written hypothesis naming what improves, for whom, and roughly by how much. |
| Scalable | Hypotheses are explicitly falsifiable, and the team has agreed in advance to publish results that disprove them. |

- **If Not ready:** Write a value hypothesis per priority scenario before launch, so results cannot be retrofitted afterwards. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Attach a figure and a named beneficiary to the existing expectation. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### case-06 · pilot-critical

**Is at least one scenario high-frequency, daily or several times a week, for the pilot cohort?**

Habit is the mechanism. Microsoft's threshold is around 3 active days a week, or 12 days in 28, and a cohort whose only scenarios are monthly cannot physically reach it inside a pilot window.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Every scenario is monthly or rarer, so the cohort cannot reach a usage habit inside the pilot window. |
| Emerging | There is a plausible daily scenario, but nobody has confirmed the cohort actually does it that often. |
| Ready | At least one scenario is confirmed daily or several times weekly for most of the cohort. |
| Scalable | Multiple high-frequency scenarios exist across several roles, so habit does not depend on a single task. |

- **If Not ready:** Add at least one genuinely high-frequency scenario. Microsoft's habit threshold of roughly 3 active days a week cannot be reached on monthly tasks. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Confirm the frequency assumption with the cohort before relying on it. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [AI Adoption Score, habit threshold](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/adoption/ai-adoption-score)

### case-07

**Does each scenario have a named business owner who cares whether it works?**

Scenarios owned by the project get abandoned the moment they are inconvenient. Scenarios owned by a person who benefits get fixed.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Scenarios belong to the project. No business person is accountable for whether any of them works. |
| Emerging | A business owner is named per scenario but has not agreed to the role and has no time allocated. |
| Ready | Each priority scenario has a named business owner who has accepted the role and will review the result. |
| Scalable | Scenario owners drive their own scenarios and come to the project team for help, rather than the reverse. |

- **If Not ready:** Assign a named business owner to each priority scenario before launch and secure their acceptance. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Confirm the named owners have accepted and hold time to review results. *(organisation, during-pilot)*

## 3. People readiness

*Whether the cohort can and will engage, and whether the conditions exist for their behaviour to mean anything.*

### ppl-01

**Does the pilot cohort know they are in a pilot, and did they opt in rather than get assigned?**

Assigned participants generate compliance rather than usage. It also changes what the telemetry means, because low usage from a conscripted cohort tells you nothing about Copilot.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The cohort has not been told they are in a pilot, or was assigned to it without being asked. |
| Emerging | The cohort was told, but participation was framed as an instruction rather than a choice. |
| Ready | The cohort knows they are in a pilot, knows what is expected of them, and had a genuine opportunity to decline. |
| Scalable | There is more demand to join than there are places, and a waiting list exists. |

- **If Not ready:** Tell the cohort they are in a pilot and what is expected of them before licences arrive. Conscripted participants produce compliance data, not adoption data. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Offer a genuine opt-out and confirm who remains, so that low usage can be read as a signal rather than as resistance. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### ppl-02

**Do you have a read on how the cohort currently feels about AI at work, captured rather than assumed?**

This is the people-side baseline and, like the value baseline, it is unrecoverable once licences land. A short pre-survey now is what lets you show a sentiment shift later. Pairs with meas-10.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody has asked the cohort how they feel about AI at work, and the plan assumes enthusiasm. |
| Emerging | Sentiment is known anecdotally from a few conversations, not captured in any repeatable form. |
| Ready | A short pre-pilot survey has been run with the cohort and the results are recorded. |
| Scalable | The survey uses questions that will be repeated at the end, and comparable data exists from previous change efforts. |

- **If Not ready:** Run a short sentiment and confidence survey with the cohort before launch. This baseline cannot be recovered once licences land. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Convert the anecdotal read into a recorded short survey that can be repeated at the end. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### ppl-03 · pilot-critical

**Have works council, employee representatives or unions been engaged where consultation is required?**

In much of Europe this is a legal gate rather than a courtesy. Discovering it in week two stops the pilot dead, and unwinding it takes weeks.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Consultation is required and has not started, or nobody has checked whether it is required at all. |
| Emerging | Consultation has started but is not concluded, and the start date assumes it will conclude in time. |
| Ready | Required consultation is concluded and the outcome is recorded. |
| Scalable | Employee representatives are engaged as partners in the design, not merely consulted on the decision. |

- **If Not ready:** Establish whether works council or employee representative consultation is legally required, and complete it before launch. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Conclude the open consultation and record the outcome before committing to a start date. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

### ppl-04 · pilot-critical

**Is there a clear, communicated position on how Copilot usage data will and will not be used, particularly at individual level?**

You are about to instrument people's work. If nobody has said "this will not appear in your performance review", a portion of the cohort will rationally minimise their footprint. Pairs with meas-08, which is the decision behind this communication.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nothing has been said, and the cohort has not asked because they assume the worst. |
| Emerging | An internal intention exists about individual-level data but has not been communicated to the cohort. |
| Ready | A clear statement has been communicated covering what is measured, at what granularity, and explicitly what it will not be used for. |
| Scalable | The statement is backed by the actual reporting configuration, so the commitment is enforced rather than promised. |

- **If Not ready:** State publicly, before launch, how Copilot usage data will and will not be used, and specifically whether it reaches individual performance conversations. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Communicate the existing internal position to the cohort rather than leaving them to infer it. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### ppl-05 · pilot-critical

**Does the cohort have the slack to learn during the pilot, or are they running at full utilisation?**

Learning a new tool costs time before it saves any. A cohort with no headroom reverts under the first deadline, and you will misread that as the tool failing.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The cohort is at or above full utilisation, with no time allocated for learning. |
| Emerging | Learning is expected to happen alongside an unchanged workload, in effect in people's own time. |
| Ready | Explicit time is allocated for learning and experimentation, and managers have agreed to protect it. |
| Scalable | Learning time is a standing feature of how these teams work, not a pilot-period concession. |

- **If Not ready:** Allocate protected learning time and get managers to agree to it, or reselect a cohort that has headroom. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Convert the expectation into named protected hours per person per week for the pilot period. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

### ppl-06 · pilot-critical

**Is the cohort representative enough that the results will generalise, or is it all volunteers and enthusiasts?**

Enthusiast-only pilots always succeed and predict nothing. This determines whether the pilot can answer the rollout question at all.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The cohort is entirely self-selected enthusiasts, or a single unrepresentative team. |
| Emerging | The cohort skews strongly towards volunteers and early adopters, with no deliberate spread. |
| Ready | The cohort deliberately spans roles, seniority and attitude, and includes some sceptics. |
| Scalable | The cohort is a designed sample whose results the organisation has agreed in advance to treat as representative. |

- **If Not ready:** Broaden the cohort to include people who did not volunteer, or the pilot cannot carry a rollout decision. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Deliberately add sceptics and mid-tenure staff so the results can carry a rollout decision. *(joint, before-pilot)*

### ppl-07

**Do people see Copilot as relevant to their actual role, or as something for knowledge workers elsewhere in the building?**

Perceived irrelevance is a harder blocker than scepticism, because sceptics at least engage while the unconvinced simply do not show up.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The cohort sees Copilot as something for other roles and cannot name a task of their own that it would touch. |
| Emerging | People accept it might be useful in principle but cannot connect it to their own week. |
| Ready | Most of the cohort can name a specific task of their own they expect Copilot to help with. |
| Scalable | People are already proposing uses the project team had not thought of. |

- **If Not ready:** Run role-specific demonstrations using the cohort's own work before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Have each person name one task of their own before launch, so their first use is already chosen. *(coach, before-pilot)*

## 4. Skills

*Whether people can get a good result safely, and whether the environment lets Copilot see the work in the first place.*

### skill-01

**Can the cohort write a prompt that supplies context, and do they understand why a bare one-line request underperforms?**

The floor skill. Nearly all "Copilot gave me rubbish" reports in early pilots trace back to context-free prompting, and it is cheap to fix before launch rather than after people have formed a verdict.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The cohort has had no prompting guidance, and the plan assumes that typing a question is enough. |
| Emerging | People have seen example prompts but have not practised, and cannot explain why context changes the result. |
| Ready | The cohort has practised prompting on their own work and can iterate on a poor result rather than abandoning it. |
| Scalable | People routinely refine and reuse prompts, and can teach the practice to someone else. |

- **If Not ready:** Run prompting practice on the cohort's own scenarios before launch, rather than a generic demonstration. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Convert the example prompts into a hands-on practice session using real work. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### skill-02 · pilot-critical

**Does the cohort know how to verify Copilot output, and can they say which of their tasks demand verification before the output is used?**

Verification is the skill that makes the accountability standard operable. Note the second half: knowing how to check matters less than knowing when you must.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody has been told to check Copilot output, and people cannot say which of their tasks would be harmed by an error. |
| Emerging | People know they should check output in general, but cannot distinguish work that requires verification from work that does not. |
| Ready | The cohort can name which of their tasks require verification before the output is used, and knows how to carry that check out. |
| Scalable | Verification is a visible norm, and people routinely flag AI-assisted work for review where the accountability standard requires it. |

- **If Not ready:** Train verification alongside prompting before launch, tied explicitly to the accountability standard from lead-05. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Have each role classify its own tasks by whether output requires verification before use. *(joint, before-pilot)*

### skill-03 · pilot-critical

**Do people know what they may and may not put into Copilot, in terms of their own organisation's data classification?**

Sits at the intersection of skill and risk. A cohort that is guessing produces either a data incident or paralysed under-use, and both are avoidable with one short briefing.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | People do not know what they may put into Copilot, and no guidance has been issued. |
| Emerging | A general data policy exists but has not been translated into what it means for Copilot specifically. |
| Ready | The cohort has clear, Copilot-specific guidance on what data may be used, expressed in their own classification terms. |
| Scalable | The guidance is reinforced by technical controls such as sensitivity labels, so the rule is enforced rather than remembered. |

- **If Not ready:** Issue Copilot-specific data handling guidance before launch. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Translate the general policy into Copilot-specific rules in the cohort's own classification language. *(joint, before-pilot)*

### skill-04

**Is there capability in the specific applications the scenarios need, or only general Copilot Chat literacy?**

Copilot in Excel and Copilot in Word behave very differently. Chat-only skilling strands scenario owners the moment a scenario touches a document or a spreadsheet.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Skilling covers Copilot Chat only, while the scenarios depend on Copilot inside Word, Excel or Teams. |
| Emerging | Application-specific capability is assumed but has neither been taught nor checked. |
| Ready | The cohort has been skilled in the specific applications their scenarios require. |
| Scalable | Application capability is maintained per role, and new applications are added as scenarios expand. |

- **If Not ready:** Extend skilling to cover the specific applications the scenarios need. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Confirm application capability with a short practical check rather than assuming it. *(coach, during-pilot)*

### skill-05

**Do people have a sense of when not to reach for Copilot at all?**

Over-application produces worse results than non-use and burns credibility faster. Distinct from verification, because this is about task selection rather than output checking.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The message has been to use it for everything, with no guidance on where it is a poor fit. |
| Emerging | People sense there are limits but hold no shared view of where those limits lie. |
| Ready | The cohort can name task types where they would not use Copilot, and say why. |
| Scalable | Judgement about fit is discussed openly, and people share where it did not work as readily as where it did. |

- **If Not ready:** Include where not to use Copilot in the launch briefing. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Collect and publish the cohort's own list of poor-fit tasks during the pilot. *(coach, during-pilot)*

### skill-06 · pilot-critical

**Is the underlying M365 hygiene in place that Copilot depends on, such as files in SharePoint or OneDrive rather than local drives, and meetings actually transcribed?**

Copilot can only reason over what it can see. A cohort that emails attachments around and never records meetings has capped Copilot before anyone types a prompt. Pairs with case-04.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Work lives on local drives or in email attachments, and meetings are neither recorded nor transcribed. |
| Emerging | Practice is mixed. Some teams work in SharePoint or OneDrive and others do not, so results will vary unpredictably across the cohort. |
| Ready | The cohort's working content is in SharePoint or OneDrive, and relevant meetings are transcribed as a matter of course. |
| Scalable | Content hygiene is managed deliberately, and the organisation treats it as a precondition for AI rather than a filing preference. |

- **If Not ready:** Fix content hygiene for the cohort before launch, or accept that Copilot has been capped before anyone types a prompt. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Bring the lagging teams up to the same content practice, so results are comparable across the cohort. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

### skill-07

**Is there a way for the cohort to find and reuse prompts that worked for their colleagues?**

Peer prompt reuse is the fastest observed route from novice to habitual use, and it costs almost nothing to set up.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no way to see what has worked for anyone else. Everyone starts from zero. |
| Emerging | Prompts are shared ad hoc in chat threads, where they are lost within days. |
| Ready | There is a shared, findable place where working prompts are collected against the cohort's scenarios. |
| Scalable | The prompt collection is curated and actively used, and the best prompts are promoted into the scenario definitions. |

- **If Not ready:** Set up a shared prompt collection before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Move ad hoc prompt sharing into a findable, persistent place. *(coach, during-pilot)*

### skill-08

**Is there a plan to refresh skills as Copilot capabilities change?**

The product moves faster than most training cycles. Skilling delivered once decays into folklore within a couple of quarters.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Skilling is a one-off event with no plan beyond it. |
| Emerging | Refreshers are intended but unscheduled and unowned. |
| Ready | There is a scheduled route for keeping skills current as capabilities change, with a named owner. |
| Scalable | Skill refresh is business as usual and tracks the Microsoft release cadence. |

- **If Not ready:** Plan how skills stay current past launch, with dates and an owner. *(coach, before-rollout)*
- **If Emerging:** Put dates and an owner against the intended refreshers. *(organisation, before-rollout)*

### skill-09

**Has anyone actually measured the cohort's starting skill level, rather than assuming it?**

Skilling designed against an assumed baseline usually pitches too high. This is also the cheapest way to evidence a skills gain later.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody has checked what the cohort can already do, and skilling has been designed on assumption. |
| Emerging | Starting level is known impressionistically for a few individuals, not for the cohort. |
| Ready | The cohort's starting skill level has been checked, and skilling is pitched against what was found. |
| Scalable | The same check will be repeated at the end, so skill gain is evidenced rather than claimed. |

- **If Not ready:** Run a short skills check before designing the skilling. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Extend the impressionistic read into a quick check across the whole cohort. *(coach, before-pilot)*

## 5. Enablement ecosystem

*Whether the support around the cohort exists in practice rather than on a slide.*

### enab-01 · pilot-critical

**Are champions identified by name, and have their managers agreed to the time it will take?**

Microsoft's planning checklist makes champion selection a core task. The second half is where it usually fails: champions volunteer, their day job does not shrink, and the role quietly evaporates by week three.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | No champions have been identified. |
| Emerging | Champions are named but their managers have not agreed to the time, so the role depends on goodwill. |
| Ready | Champions are named, have accepted, and their managers have agreed to specific hours. |
| Scalable | The champion role is recognised in how their contribution is assessed, not only in their calendar. |

- **If Not ready:** Identify champions by name before launch and secure their managers' agreement to the hours. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Get manager agreement to specific champion hours. A champion role funded from goodwill evaporates by week three. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption planning checklist](https://adoption.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/essential-guide/plan/)

### enab-02

**Is champion coverage adequate for the cohort, with at least one reachable champion inside each team?**

Champions work through proximity. A champion two floors away in a different function is an org chart entry, not a support network.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is one champion for the whole cohort, or none within reach of most teams. |
| Emerging | Champion coverage is uneven, and some teams have nobody nearby. |
| Ready | Every team in the cohort has at least one champion they can reach easily. |
| Scalable | Coverage is planned against the rollout population, not only the pilot cohort. |

- **If Not ready:** Recruit champions until every team in the cohort has one within reach. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Fill the coverage gaps for the teams currently without a nearby champion. *(joint, before-pilot)*

### enab-03

**Is there a communications plan with an actual launch moment, rather than a single announcement email?**

A launch moment creates a shared starting line, which is what makes peer learning possible. Staggered silent enablement produces isolated individuals.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The plan is a single announcement email, or nothing at all. |
| Emerging | There is a communications plan but no shared starting moment, so the cohort begins in isolation from one another. |
| Ready | There is a communications plan with a launch moment the whole cohort attends, and a follow-up rhythm after it. |
| Scalable | Communications are planned across the pilot and into rollout, with the sponsor visible at each milestone. |

- **If Not ready:** Build a communications plan with an actual launch moment. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Add a launch moment the whole cohort attends together. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### enab-04

**Is there one obvious place people go for help, prompts and resources?**

Scattered resources mean people ask nobody. Microsoft's guidance is explicit about having one place to share resources, host training and highlight what is working.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Resources are scattered across email, chat threads and personal drives, so people ask nobody. |
| Emerging | A hub exists but is incomplete or hard to find, and people do not use it. |
| Ready | There is one obvious place holding help, prompts, training and contacts, and the cohort knows where it is. |
| Scalable | The hub is maintained, and its content reflects what the cohort actually asks for. |

- **If Not ready:** Create one findable place for help, prompts and resources before launch. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Complete the hub and point every communication at it so it becomes the default. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption planning checklist](https://adoption.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/essential-guide/plan/)

### enab-05 · pilot-critical

**Is training actually scheduled with people enrolled, rather than "available on demand"?**

On-demand training is completed by the people who least need it. A booked session with a date is the only version that reaches the median user.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Training is on demand only, or has not been arranged at all. |
| Emerging | Sessions exist but are optional and unscheduled, so attendance is unknown. |
| Ready | Training is scheduled with named people enrolled and dates in calendars before launch. |
| Scalable | Enrolment is tracked and followed up, and non-attendance is noticed and addressed. |

- **If Not ready:** Schedule training with named enrolment before launch. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Convert the optional sessions into scheduled slots with named enrolment. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

### enab-06

**Is there an onboarding route for people who join the cohort after launch?**

Every pilot gains people. Without a route in, latecomers get licences and no context, and they are the ones who show up as non-users in your data.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no route in for anyone joining after launch. Latecomers get a licence and nothing else. |
| Emerging | Onboarding for latecomers is improvised by whoever happens to be available. |
| Ready | There is a defined onboarding route for people joining after launch, with an owner. |
| Scalable | The route is self-service and works at rollout scale without the project team. |

- **If Not ready:** Define an onboarding route for latecomers. *(coach, during-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Give the improvised onboarding an owner and a written route. *(coach, during-pilot)*

### enab-07 · pilot-critical

**Is there a named person accountable for enablement day to day, distinct from the executive sponsor?**

Sponsors open doors, they do not run programmes. Pilots with a sponsor but no operational owner drift, because nobody's calendar owns the work. The Ready anchor also carries service desk readiness and champion readiness, since this owner is precisely who would have arranged both.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody owns enablement day to day. The sponsor is expected to run it, or it falls between roles. |
| Emerging | Someone is doing the work informally alongside their job, with the role neither recognised nor resourced. |
| Ready | A named person owns enablement day to day with allocated time, and has already briefed the service desk on handling Copilot tickets and trained the champions ahead of the cohort. |
| Scalable | The role is permanent rather than pilot-scoped, and continues into rollout with a defined remit. |

- **If Not ready:** Name a person accountable for enablement day to day, distinct from the sponsor, and give them allocated time. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Recognise and resource the role formally, and have that owner brief the service desk and train the champions before the cohort starts. *(organisation, before-pilot)*

## 6. Measurement

*Whether the pilot is set up to produce an answer that a decision can be based on.*

### meas-01 · pilot-critical

**Have you agreed what "adopted" means numerically, before the pilot starts?**

Microsoft gives you defensible definitions off the shelf: a habitual user is active in at least 9 of 12 weeks, a power user adds 15 or more actions a week, and the AI Adoption Score habit target is 12 active days in 28. Picking one in advance stops the goalposts moving once results arrive.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | No definition of adopted exists, so success will be argued rather than measured. |
| Emerging | There is a vague intent such as "people using it regularly", with no threshold attached to it. |
| Ready | A specific threshold has been agreed in advance, for example Microsoft's habitual user definition of active in at least 9 of 12 weeks. |
| Scalable | The chosen definition is applied consistently across all reporting, and the organisation has agreed not to move it once results arrive. |

- **If Not ready:** Agree a numeric definition of adopted before launch, using one of the published Microsoft thresholds rather than inventing one. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Attach a specific threshold to the existing intent and write it down before any results arrive. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [AI Adoption Score, habit threshold](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/adoption/ai-adoption-score)

### meas-02 · pilot-critical

**Can someone actually access and read the Copilot reporting, with the licences and roles that requires?**

The Viva Insights adoption report needs an Insights Analyst role and Power BI Desktop. Discovering that in week six means your pilot ran blind.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Nobody has checked who can see Copilot reporting, and no role or licence has been arranged. |
| Emerging | The reporting exists in principle but nobody has opened it, and the required roles are unconfirmed. |
| Ready | A named person holds the roles and licences required, and has already produced a report from live data. |
| Scalable | Reporting is routine, and more than one person can produce and interpret it. |

- **If Not ready:** Confirm who will read the reporting and arrange their roles and licences before launch. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Have the named person produce one report from live data before launch, to prove the access actually works. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption report, usage level definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/advanced/analyst/templates/microsoft-365-copilot-adoption)

### meas-03

**Is there a review cadence with a decision attached to each review, rather than a report nobody acts on?**

Data without a decision point is theatre. This is also how mid-pilot course corrections happen while there is still pilot left to correct.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no plan to review pilot data during the pilot. |
| Emerging | Reviews are planned but with no decision attached, so they inform nobody. |
| Ready | A review cadence is set, and each review has a named decision it feeds. |
| Scalable | Reviews have visibly changed the course of previous initiatives, so the cadence has teeth. |

- **If Not ready:** Set a review cadence with a decision attached to each review, so course correction happens while there is still pilot left to correct. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Attach a specific decision to each scheduled review. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### meas-04

**Is there a route for qualitative feedback, including "this is not working for me", that reaches someone who can act on it?**

Moved here deliberately from People readiness. Drop-off is your most valuable signal, and telemetry only shows you that it happened, never why.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no route for qualitative feedback. Telemetry is the only signal. |
| Emerging | Feedback is welcomed in principle but has no channel and no owner, so it reaches nobody. |
| Ready | There is a named channel for qualitative feedback, negative included, with someone accountable for responding. |
| Scalable | Feedback visibly changes the programme, and the cohort can point to something that changed because they said so. |

- **If Not ready:** Create a route for qualitative feedback that reaches someone who can act on it. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Give the feedback channel a name, a location and an owner. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### meas-05

**Have you decided how you will capture value that telemetry cannot see, such as output quality, confidence or reduced drudgery?**

Usage counts prove habit, not worth. Copilot's most reported benefits are frequently invisible to action counts.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The only planned measure is usage counts. |
| Emerging | There is an intent to capture qualitative value but no method and no instrument. |
| Ready | A method exists for capturing value telemetry cannot see, such as quality, confidence or reduced drudgery. |
| Scalable | Qualitative and quantitative measures are reported together, and neither is treated as the whole story. |

- **If Not ready:** Decide how you will capture value that telemetry cannot see. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Choose the instrument, whether survey, interview or diary, and schedule it. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot impact report](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/advanced/analyst/templates/microsoft-365-copilot-impact)

### meas-06

**Is there a plan to collect success stories while they are fresh, rather than reconstructing them at the end?**

Specific stories are what carry a go decision to a steering committee. Reconstructed ones are vague and unconvincing at exactly the moment you need them to be sharp.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no plan to collect stories. Anything useful will be reconstructed from memory at the end. |
| Emerging | Stories will be gathered at the end of the pilot rather than as they happen. |
| Ready | There is a running route for capturing specific examples as they occur, in the person's own words. |
| Scalable | Stories are actively used during the pilot to recruit and reassure the rest of the organisation. |

- **If Not ready:** Set up a way to capture examples while they are fresh. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Move story capture from the end of the pilot to a running collection during it. *(coach, during-pilot)*

### meas-07 · pilot-critical

**Is it agreed what would count as a failed pilot, not only what would count as success?**

Pilots without a defined failure condition never fail, they get extended. Writing the condition down in advance is what makes the eventual decision credible.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Only success has been described. There is no condition under which this pilot would be judged to have failed. |
| Emerging | Failure is discussed informally but has not been written down or agreed. |
| Ready | A written failure condition has been agreed in advance, alongside the success condition. |
| Scalable | The organisation has previously stopped something on this basis, so the condition is credible rather than decorative. |

- **If Not ready:** Agree in writing what would count as a failed pilot, before it starts. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Write down and get agreement on the failure condition that is currently only discussed. *(coach, before-pilot)*

### meas-08

**Have you decided the reporting granularity and the privacy line, for example group level rather than individual level?**

This is the decision, where ppl-04 is the communication of it. Both are needed and they fail separately, because many organisations communicate a reassurance they have not actually designed the reporting to honour.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | Reporting granularity has not been decided, so individual-level reporting is the default. |
| Emerging | There is a preference for group-level reporting but it has been neither decided nor configured. |
| Ready | Granularity is decided and documented, including a minimum group size for aggregated views. |
| Scalable | The decision is enforced in the reporting configuration itself, so it cannot be quietly bypassed. |

- **If Not ready:** Decide the reporting granularity and privacy line before configuring anything, including a minimum group size for aggregated views. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Turn the preference into a documented decision and configure the reports to match it. *(organisation, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption report, usage level definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/advanced/analyst/templates/microsoft-365-copilot-adoption)

### meas-09 · pilot-critical

**Is the pilot long enough for a habit to form before you judge it?**

Microsoft's consistency thresholds are measured over 12 weeks. A four-week pilot cannot produce a habitual user by Microsoft's own definition, so it structurally cannot answer the question you are asking it.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | The pilot is scheduled to be judged after a few weeks, which is too short for a usage habit to form. |
| Emerging | The duration is undecided, or has been set by an external deadline rather than by what the measurement requires. |
| Ready | The pilot runs long enough for consistency to be measurable, in line with the 12-week window Microsoft's usage levels use. |
| Scalable | Duration is set by the measurement requirement, and the decision point is protected against being pulled forward. |

- **If Not ready:** Extend the pilot to a length at which a habit can form, or accept that it cannot answer the question you are asking it. *(joint, before-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Set the duration from the measurement requirement rather than from an external deadline. *(coach, before-pilot)*
- Source: [Microsoft 365 Copilot adoption report, usage level definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/advanced/analyst/templates/microsoft-365-copilot-adoption)

### meas-10

**Will you re-measure the people-side baseline at the end, using the same instrument you used at the start?**

Pairs with ppl-02. A sentiment shift is often the clearest result a pilot produces, but only if both measurements use the same questions.

| Level | You know it is this when |
|---|---|
| Not ready | There is no plan to re-measure anything on the people side at the end. |
| Emerging | A closing measurement is intended but will use different questions from the opening one. |
| Ready | The closing measurement repeats the opening instrument, so the comparison is valid. |
| Scalable | The comparison is planned to be repeated at rollout, giving a trend rather than two isolated points. |

- **If Not ready:** Plan to repeat the opening people-side measurement at the end, using the same questions. *(coach, during-pilot)*
- **If Emerging:** Align the closing instrument with the opening one so the two are actually comparable. *(coach, during-pilot)*
